Ridgewend privacy policy

This is the privacy policy for Ridgewend, a hiking map and recorder. It covers the Android app, application id io.github.felipebatistellaui.trail, and the same app opened as a web page. It describes what the software does with information. Ridgewend has no server of its own, and this policy does not invent one.

Effective date: 1 November 2026

Privacy contact: tellabatisapps@outlook.com

This policy is published at https://felipebatistella-ui.github.io/ridgewend-privacy/.

Who is responsible

Felipe, an individual developer based in the United Kingdom, is the data controller for personal data that Ridgewend processes. The app does not name a company. No data protection officer has been appointed.

For a privacy request, write to tellabatisapps@outlook.com.

What Ridgewend does

You can look at a topographic map and a street map, plan a walk, record a GPS track, follow a saved route, attach photos to the map, and read a weather forecast. Saved hikes and photos stay in storage on this device. The app does not create an account, and there is no account to delete.

What the app does not do

Ridgewend does not sell personal data. It does not show advertisements, and it does not use an advertising identifier. It does not include an analytics or crash-reporting library, and it does not send the developer a usage log or a crash log. It does not ask you to sign in. It does not read your contacts, your messages, your microphone, or the list of other apps on the device. It does not set its own cookies.

Location

On this device

Ridgewend reads precise GPS when you use a feature that needs it: the location button, starting or resuming a recording, following a saved route, or choosing My location while planning. A reading contains latitude, longitude, altitude when the device has it, accuracy, and the time. Speed and direction of travel are used when the device provides them, to point the arrow on the map. A compass heading, when the device provides one, is used only to rotate that arrow. The compass heading is not sent off the device.

Until you accept it, the app shows a short notice before it starts GPS and before it sends the map area. Continue stores that acceptance on the device and then starts GPS. The phone or the browser then asks for its own location permission. Not now does not store the acceptance, does not start GPS, and does not ask for that permission, so the notice can appear again later. If you refuse the system permission, GPS does not run. Map tiles still wait for Continue, as described below.

You can open the map without GPS. On a fresh install the map opens on a default view in the Alps, around latitude 46.8 and longitude 8.2, not on you. After you move the map, the app remembers the centre and zoom on this device only.

Points stored in a recording are kept to six decimal places, which is finer than a metre, and height is kept to a tenth of a metre when height is present. A fix whose reported accuracy is worse than 35 metres is not added to a recording. Battery saver is off unless you turn it on. It still uses precise GPS. On Android it asks for a new fix about every 20 metres of movement instead of about every 5 metres. On Android and on the web, a point is stored only after you have moved about 15 metres instead of about 4 metres.

Android, including with the screen off

On Android, GPS keeps running with the screen off in three cases: a recording is running, a recording is auto-paused, or you are following a route. The app does this with a foreground service of type location and a notification that stays visible. The notification title is Ridgewend. The text is "Recording your hike", or "Following" and the name of the route. The notification channel is named Hike recording.

The app does not declare Android's separate background-location permission (ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION), the choice often shown as Allow all the time. Screen-off tracking is the location notification, not that permission.

Auto-pause is on unless you turn it off. After about two minutes without movement the timer pauses, and the location service keeps running so the timer can resume when you walk on more than about 20 metres. A manual pause, when you are not also following a route, stops the screen-off service. GPS can then continue only while you are using the app. Finishing the recording, or stopping following, ends the screen-off service.

If the system closes the app, an unfinished recording remains on the phone. GPS starts again the next time you open Ridgewend, if that recording was still running or auto-paused. Switching the phone on does not start GPS by itself.

The first time screen-off tracking starts, the app asks for permission to post notifications, so it can show the recording notification. If you leave a route you are following, it can also show an on-device alert with the distance off the route and the route name. That alert is not sent to a server.

Centring the map on yourself does not upload the track. After you have tapped Continue it does request map tiles for that view and, when height correction is on and you are online, a height correction for the current point. Following a route compares your position with the line already stored on the device. It does not send that line back to the routing server.

The web page

In a browser there is no screen-off location service. GPS uses the browser's location feature while the page is open. During a recording the page asks the browser to keep the screen awake. Closing the page stops that.

When a position leaves the device

Felipe does not receive your track. There is nowhere in Ridgewend for it to be uploaded as a matter of course.

A position leaves the device only in the requests described in this policy, and only after you tap Continue on the location notice. Until then the app does not request map tiles, routes, search, weather, place icons, or height correction. Each of those requests also carries your IP address, because that is how the phone reaches the other server. The app does not attach a cookie, an account, the hike's name, or your photos. The page does not set a User-Agent header. Browsers forbid that, and Ridgewend does not work around the ban. On the website, a tile request sends a Referer that is the site you opened. That is what the OpenStreetMap tile policy asks of a website. On Android, the WebView user agent also has Ridgewend/1 (io.github.felipebatistellaui.trail) added by the app shell, not by the page script, and the Referer is https://felipebatistella-ui.github.io. That Android origin is a label set in the app. It is not, by itself, a visit to GitHub.

The notice says map tiles and other requests that show where the map is, or where you are, wait until you tap Continue, and that the saved track stays on the phone unless you share or export it. Tapping Not now leaves those requests unsent. Stop sending map location, under More, forgets Continue and stops those requests. It does not delete your hikes or photos. Tiles already saved on the device can still show. Allow map requests opens the notice again. You have to tap Continue to start the requests once more. If the phone or browser tells the app that GPS permission is off, the app explains that. Keep the map then stops location completely until you turn it back on: no GPS reading, no centring on you, and no coordinates sent, including map tiles of where you are. Tiles already saved can still show. Your hikes and photos stay. Location stays off until you tap Allow map requests and then Continue.

Map tiles

The map has two layers. Looking at either layer tells that layer's server which square of the map is on screen. Your IP address goes with the request. A tile request does not contain your name.

No tile is requested until you tap Continue. Zoomed out, the square is a wide area. From about zoom level 15 upward, one tile covers less than 3 square kilometres, so it is a precise location of whatever the map is showing. That is your GPS position only when the map is centred on you. Tiles for the default Alps view are not your position. If the app restored a map centre from last time, tiles for that place are requested only after you tap Continue.

The topographic layer uses OpenTopoMap. The app requests tiles from a.tile.opentopomap.org, b.tile.opentopomap.org, and c.tile.opentopomap.org. The project describes itself at OpenTopoMap. That page does not publish a separate privacy notice or a retention period, so this policy does not invent one.

The street layer uses tiles from tile.openstreetmap.org, operated by the OpenStreetMap Foundation in the United Kingdom. The Foundation's privacy policy is here, and its note for services and tile users is here. The Foundation says it is an independent controller and does not sign a processor contract for this kind of use. It says personal data other than data associated with a tile request is stored in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands. Standard map tiles are served from a global cache, so the server that answers can be outside the United Kingdom.

Tiles you have already viewed are kept on the device, in a cache named trail-tiles-v1. The app trims that cache to about 15,000 tiles by dropping older ones. Save visible area downloads more topographic tiles for the area on screen, from OpenTopoMap only, and at most 64 tiles for one save. It does not bulk-download street tiles from the OpenStreetMap tile server. Clear saved maps deletes that cache.

On the web, a service worker caches the app's pages and the tiles you view. It does not contact OpenTopoMap, the OpenStreetMap tile server, Open-Meteo, brouter.de, Nominatim, or Photon until you have tapped Continue. That choice is stored where the worker can read it. A tile already in the cache can still be shown. When this worker is updated it takes over straight away, so an older copy does not keep fetching those hosts. The Android app does not register that service worker. It still caches tiles inside the app. The map keeps the OpenStreetMap and OpenTopoMap credits on screen.

Routes, search, weather, and height

None of the requests in this section are sent until you tap Continue on the location notice.

Height correction

Height correction is on unless you turn it off under More. When it is on, and you have tapped Continue, the app sends coordinates to Open-Meteo. When it is off, it does not send them.

While GPS is running and the device is online, that request is your current latitude and longitude, to five decimal places (about a metre), sent to api.open-meteo.com to read the ground height. It skips the request when the last one was under two minutes ago and you have moved less than about 200 metres. After two minutes, or after you have moved about 200 metres, it can send the point again. The result is used to correct the GPS altitude, including later when you are offline.

When you save a recording, and again when you open the app or the device comes online, the app sends positions from that saved hike to the same address, if the hike has not already been corrected and the setting is still on. A long hike is thinned to roughly 1,500 positions, sent up to 100 at a time. The corrected heights are written back into the hike on the device. The hike's name and photos are not included.

This service is Open-Meteo, operated by OpenMeteo GmbH in Switzerland. Open-Meteo says it is an independent controller, and that server logs which can contain coordinates are deleted after 90 days. Their notice is in the Open-Meteo terms.

Weather

Weather is requested only when you open it. The app sends one latitude and longitude, to four decimal places (about ten metres), to the same Open-Meteo address, and asks for a three-day forecast. The point is the start of a route you are planning, the start of a saved hike, or the map centre, depending on the button you used. The forecast is shown on screen. It is not stored as its own record.

Routing

When you plan a route that follows trails, including From → To, a loop, or other route choices, the app sends the waypoint coordinates, to six decimal places, to brouter.de, together with the style you chose: hiking, easy walking, or shortest. A loop can take several attempts. Asking for other routes sends several requests. If you chose My location as the start, that waypoint is your GPS position. The route that comes back stays on the device if you save it.

The operator of brouter.de says a visit is written to ordinary server logs, including IP address, time, the address requested, user agent, and the coordinates of the route you asked for. Those logs are kept for two weeks, and then for up to ten further days in the hosting provider's backups. The notice says they do not use cookies or an external tracking tool. It does not name a country. Ridgewend does not upload a custom routing profile. Their notice is the brouter.de privacy policy.

Place search

When you type a search, the words, the language set on the device, and a box around the map centre are sent to nominatim.openstreetmap.org. The box extends two degrees of latitude and longitude on each side of the centre. It is a wide area, and the centre can be worked out from it. This is the map centre, not a separate copy of the GPS fix, unless the map is centred on you. The search is not saved as a history in the app.

Nominatim is a service of the OpenStreetMap Foundation. The Foundation's privacy policy is linked in the map-tile section.

Stations and places on the map

Station search, and the place icons, send a latitude and longitude to five decimal places to photon.komoot.io, with the kind of place being looked up. Drinking water, toilets, and train stations are shown unless you turn them off. Cafés, pubs, viewpoints, parking, and bus stops are hidden unless you turn them on. Station search uses your GPS position when you ask for stations near you or for stations a walk from a start you picked. Otherwise it uses the map centre. Place icons load when you zoom in to about street level, around the map centre. They are not a continuous upload of your GPS track.

photon.komoot.io is the public Photon server provided by Komoot. Komoot's privacy notice is here. That notice describes Komoot's own service. It does not, in a form this policy can cite, state how long this public server keeps a geocoding request. Ridgewend has no separate agreement with Komoot.

Send my location

This runs only when you tap Send my location. The app builds a message with the time, the accuracy in metres, and a Google Maps link that contains your latitude and longitude to six decimal places. It does not attach the rest of your track.

On Android the message opens in the share sheet, and you choose the other app. On the web it uses the browser's share feature or, if that is missing, copies the message to the clipboard. Ridgewend does not choose the recipient and does not contact Google itself. If someone opens the Google Maps link, Google receives those coordinates under Google's own privacy policy. The app you pick receives the message under that app's terms.

Hikes, routes, and photos stored on this device

Saved routes, recorded hikes, and an unfinished recording are stored in the app's local storage on this device. A saved item has the name you gave it, the date, and the points: latitude, longitude, elevation, and time when the point has a time. A GPX file you import is read on the device and stored in the same way. Import does not upload the file.

Photos are stored in a local database in the app, named trail. Each photo is a JPEG, reduced so the long side is about 1,280 pixels, with the latitude, longitude, and time of its pin. The pin uses a GPS fix from the last two minutes when there is one. Otherwise it uses the map centre, and the app says so. On Android, taking a photo hands the shot to the phone's camera and stores the picture that comes back. The app does not ask for the Android camera permission, and it does not add the picture to your gallery. On the web, the page uses the browser's camera or file picker.

Deleting a hike removes that hike and the photos taken during it. A photo counts when its time is from the first recorded time on the hike to the last, plus one minute. A photo outside that window, and a photo on a planned route that has no times, is left in place. You can still delete a photo from its own pin.

The app also keeps, on this device only, your settings (battery saver, voice, auto-pause, height correction, large buttons, night map, distance units, and which place types to show), whether you tapped Continue on the location notice, the map layer you last used, the last map centre and zoom, the routing style, the last loop distance, and the last ground-height correction.

Stop sending map location, under More, asks you to confirm, then forgets Continue. Map tiles, routes, search, weather, place icons, height correction, and saving an area stop. Hikes and photos stay. Tiles already saved can still show. Allow map requests shows the notice again.

Delete all my data, under More, asks you to confirm, then removes hikes, the unfinished recording, photos, saved map tiles, settings, the saved map centre, the chosen map layer, the routing style, the loop distance, and the last height correction from this device. It also forgets Continue. You can still delete one hike, one photo, or only the saved tiles. Clearing the app's storage, or uninstalling the Android app, removes the rest. On the web, clearing site data for the page does the same.

Voice alerts

Voice alerts are on unless you turn them off under More. The spoken lines are navigation phrases: distance, time, turns, leaving the trail, the highest point, arrival, auto-pause, and the name of a route you are following.

On Android the app passes that text to the phone's own text-to-speech engine, with the language set to en-US. That library does not open a network connection. On the web the page uses the browser's speech synthesis. Ridgewend does not run a speech service and does not send the phrase to one. A speech engine installed on the device can have its own network use under its own terms. This app does not add that call.

Analytics and crash reports

There are none. The app does not send a usage statistic or a crash report to Felipe or to an analytics service. A failed tile, route, or weather request is handled on the device.

The app does not contact GitHub in the background to look for an update. If you tap Get the latest Android app, the Android app opens the APK address on GitHub, and the web page opens the releases page. That request does not include your hikes, photos, or GPS track. GitHub receives ordinary connection data, including your IP address. GitHub's privacy statement is here.

On the web only, the page loads the Leaflet map library from cdnjs.cloudflare.com. That request does not include your location. The Android build copies Leaflet into the app and does not contact cdnjs. Cloudflare's privacy policy is here.

Backups

Android automatic backup is off. The app is built with android:allowBackup set to false, and the release build checks that this remains false. Android backup does not copy the app's storage of hikes and photos off the phone.

You can make your own backup. The file is JSON and contains your saved hikes and routes, the photo pins, and the photos. On Android the app writes that file into its private cache and opens the share sheet. A copy can remain in that cache until the cache is cleared. On the web the file downloads to the place you choose. Restore reads a file you pick and merges it into this device. The app does not upload that file to a Ridgewend server. After you share or save it, the other service's terms apply.

Exporting one hike as GPX writes the name, positions, elevation, and times into a file, by the same download or share sheet. On Android the cache note above applies to that file as well.

Permissions

The Android app declares these permissions.

  • Internet, so it can request tiles, routes, search results, weather, height, and, if you ask, the app download.
  • Precise location and approximate location. The location library declares both. The positions the app uses are the precise GPS readings described above.
  • A foreground service, and a foreground service of type location, for the screen-off notification while you record or follow a route.
  • Notifications, for that ongoing notification and for an off-trail alert.
  • Vibration, for a short vibration on a turn or an off-trail alert. The vibration is not stored.

The app does not declare a wake lock, a boot receiver, or an exact-alarm permission. Screen-off GPS uses the location foreground service and its notification. It does not take a wake lock. The app does not start GPS when the phone boots, and it does not schedule an alarm. The notification library does declare those three, and the build removes them so they are not in the installed app. On the web, separately, the page asks the browser to keep the screen on during a recording. That is not the Android wake-lock permission.

The app does not declare a camera permission, a microphone permission, a gallery permission, the background-location permission, or an advertising identifier.

In the browser, the page can ask for location, for a photo, for a screen wake lock during a recording, and, if sharing is unavailable, to copy a location message to the clipboard. The compass heading stays on the device.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

On the device, hikes, photos, settings, and cached tiles remain until you delete them, clear the app's storage or the page's site data, or uninstall the Android app. The tile cache is also trimmed to about 15,000 tiles. Felipe does not keep a copy of what remains on the device. He cannot open it remotely, and he has no server archive of it to delete.

Coordinate requests are not stored by Felipe after the reply arrives. Ridgewend cannot delete data these providers hold. There is no control in the app that erases their logs.

  • Open-Meteo, operated by OpenMeteo GmbH, receives coordinates for height and weather. Their notice is the Open-Meteo terms. It says logs that can contain coordinates are deleted after 90 days. Ridgewend cannot delete those logs.
  • The operator of brouter.de receives the routing coordinates. Their notice is the brouter.de privacy policy. It says those logs are kept for two weeks, plus up to ten days in the host's backups. Ridgewend cannot delete those logs.
  • The OpenStreetMap Foundation receives street-map tile requests and Nominatim searches. Their notice is the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy. Ridgewend cannot delete what the Foundation keeps.
  • OpenTopoMap receives topographic tile requests. The project page is OpenTopoMap. It does not publish a separate privacy policy or a retention period. Ridgewend cannot delete what OpenTopoMap keeps.
  • Komoot provides the Photon server at photon.komoot.io, which receives coordinates for stations and place icons. Komoot's notice is the Komoot privacy policy. That notice describes Komoot's own service and does not state a retention period for this public server. Ridgewend cannot delete what that server keeps.
  • GitHub receives the connection when you tap the app download. Their notice is the GitHub privacy statement. That request does not include your hikes. Ridgewend cannot delete GitHub's logs.
  • Cloudflare receives the connection when the website loads the Leaflet library from cdnjs. Their notice is the Cloudflare privacy policy. The Android app does not make that request. Ridgewend cannot delete Cloudflare's logs.

If you send a message or a hike file to the contact email, it is used to answer you and it is not loaded into the app. Ask for that message to be deleted and it will be deleted, unless the law requires it to be kept. Write to tellabatisapps@outlook.com.

There is no account, so there is no separate account-deletion page. Removing the data on the device is how you delete what the app stored.

Children

Ridgewend is for a general audience aged 13 and over. It is not a children's app, and it is not directed at children under 13. It does not ask your age, and it has no age check. If you are under 13, do not use it.

Felipe does not knowingly collect personal data from children. He has no account list he can search. If a parent wants data removed from a device a child used, uninstall the Android app, clear the page's site data, or use Delete all my data. A parent can also write to tellabatisapps@outlook.com.

Why the law allows this

Ridgewend is offered from the United Kingdom, and UK GDPR applies to Felipe's processing. Each use below has one basis.

Consent, Article 6(1)(a). Reading precise GPS, including the Android service that continues with the screen off while you record or follow a route, and sending the map area and other coordinates described above. The app asks in its notice before any of those requests, and the operating system asks before GPS. Not now means the map does not load tiles and those requests are not sent. You can still open hikes already stored on the device. You withdraw the in-app choice with Stop sending map location. That leaves your hikes and photos in place. Delete all my data also forgets Continue, and it deletes the hikes. Clearing the app's storage forgets it too. If the app can see that the system location permission is off, it explains that. Keep the map forgets Continue. The app then does not read GPS, does not centre on you, and does not send coordinates until you tap Allow map requests and then Continue.

Necessary for the feature you asked for, Article 6(1)(b). Requesting map tiles for the area on screen, requesting a route you asked to plan, sending a search you typed, looking up stations or place icons for a search you started, fetching a forecast you opened, and storing a hike, route, or photo you chose to keep. There is no account and no separate written contract. This basis is used because those steps are what the feature does.

Legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Remembering settings and the last map view on the device, so the app opens where you left it. Keeping tiles you have already viewed, so the same ground still shows without signal. When height correction is on, sending positions to Open-Meteo, so altitude and climb are closer to height above sea level than the raw GPS figure. The interest is a usable hiking record. The hike remains on the device apart from the coordinate requests already described. You can turn height correction off under More, and you can object, as described under Your rights.

Ridgewend does not use personal data for advertising or analytics. It does not make an automated decision that has a legal effect on you, or a similarly significant effect. A suggested route and an off-trail warning are navigation aids.

The app does not ask about health, beliefs, or similar matters, and it does not try to infer them. A track you record can still pass somewhere that says something about you. That track stays on the device unless you export or share it, or unless height correction is on and sends coordinates from it.

You do not have to tap Continue to install the app. Until you do, map tiles, search, routing, weather, and height correction do not send coordinates. Without the system location permission, the live position, recording, following, and My location do not work. Without a network connection, new tiles, routing, search, weather, and height correction do not work. Hikes and tiles already on the device still open.

Other countries

Processing that stays on the device stays on the device. Felipe has no server in another country because he has no app server at all.

When a request leaves the device, the recipient is the organisation named earlier in this policy.

  • Open-Meteo is OpenMeteo GmbH in Switzerland. UK adequacy regulations cover Switzerland.
  • The OpenStreetMap Foundation is established in the United Kingdom. It says data other than tile-request data is stored in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands. UK adequacy regulations cover the EEA, which includes the Netherlands. Its tile cache is global and can be outside the United Kingdom.
  • brouter.de does not state a country in its privacy policy. The routing request is processed by that service.
  • Photon requests go to photon.komoot.io, the public server Komoot provides.
  • OpenTopoMap requests go to the tile hosts named above. The project page does not state a hosting country in a privacy notice.
  • The optional GitHub download goes to GitHub, a company based in the United States.
  • The web-only Leaflet download goes to Cloudflare. The Android app does not make that request.

These organisations receive the request from your device. They are not processors engaged by Felipe. The app contains no international-transfer contract with them. Open-Meteo and the OpenStreetMap Foundation each describe themselves as an independent controller. Their notices are the account of what happens after the request arrives.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask to see personal data, to correct it, to erase it, to restrict it, and to receive a portable copy. You can object to processing based on legitimate interests. You can withdraw consent. You can complain to the regulator. Because hikes are stored on your device, and Felipe does not hold a copy, you exercise most of these rights on the device.

  • Access. Open My hikes, the photo pins, and the backup export. Felipe cannot open the app's storage from outside the phone. He has no server copy to send, unless you have emailed him a file.
  • Correction. Rename a hike in its details, or delete it and keep the copy you want. He cannot edit the copy stored on your phone.
  • Erasure. Delete one hike, which also deletes the photos taken during it, or use Delete all my data. You can also clear saved tiles, clear the app's storage, or uninstall. He will delete a file you sent to the contact email when you ask, unless the law requires it to be kept. He cannot erase logs held by Open-Meteo, the OpenStreetMap Foundation, OpenTopoMap, brouter.de, Komoot, GitHub, or Cloudflare.
  • Restriction, and an objection to legitimate interests. Write to tellabatisapps@outlook.com. Turn height correction off under More to stop those coordinate requests. Stop sending map location stops the map and the other coordinate requests and keeps your hikes.
  • Portability. Export a hike as GPX, or export the JSON backup, and take the file where you choose.
  • Withdraw consent. Stop sending map location, or Keep the map after a GPS denial, forgets Continue and stops coordinate requests without deleting hikes. Location stays off until you tap Allow map requests and then Continue. Delete all my data also forgets Continue, and it deletes the hikes. Stop a recording or stop following to end screen-off tracking.
  • Complaint. You can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office: make a complaint.

Security

The app's own requests use HTTPS. Hikes and photos sit in the app's private storage on the device. The app does not add a separate password around that storage. Someone who can unlock the device, or someone you send a backup to, can read them. Android automatic backup of that storage is turned off. The location service is marked so that another app cannot bind to it and stop a recording.
